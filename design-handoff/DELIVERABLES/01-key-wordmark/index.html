KEY Wordmark

Three differentiated directions for the masthead of a publication that doesn’t exist yet — and is not allowed to look like an AI company. Each is a real voice, not a font swap.

I  ·  BriefWhy this matters

The wordmark is the load-bearing element of the entire KEY identity. It sits in the top-nav at 18px, in printed correspondence at 60pt, on a favicon at 16px, and on the cover of a brief that a paying member will print and keep. Whatever we choose has to read as a serious imprint at every scale.

The brief from Claude (technical lead) is unambiguous: no key icons, no lock motifs, no cyber typography, no gradients, no shadow, no container chrome. The wordmark is letterforms — three of them, uppercase, generously tracked. The differentiation between directions is the kind of seriousness each one performs.

House rules — locked

Uppercase KEY only — never Key, key, K.E.Y., KeyAI, KeyOS. Three letters, treated as a single typographic unit. Tracking ≥ +0.12em, ≤ +0.24em. Stroke weight medium-to-bold; never thin or display-condensed.

All three are set in fonts with friendly commercial licenses and stable web availability. None is a default. The differences below are not decorative — they prescribe a personality for the entire brand.

I.
Authority — the trade quarterly

Source Serif 4, Bold (700). A modern transitional serif drawn at Adobe by Frank Grießhammer; broad x-height, balanced contrast, no eccentricities. It carries weight the way a hardback book carries weight — without announcing itself. This is the voice of a serious trade journal.

Font
Source Serif 4 — variable. We use wght 700 at large sizes; can drop to 600 at body scale for a lighter nav lockup.
Tracking
+0.16em — wide enough to read as masthead, tight enough to keep KEY as a single word and not three letters.
Stroke
Medium-bold. The serifs are bracketed, not slab — so it pairs with a sans body without fighting it.
Y treatment
Untouched in V1. Source Serif 4’s Y has a structurally clean junction; a custom fork can be drawn in V2 if A is selected.
Key mark
A single 48px × 2.5px burgundy hairline, set on cap-line, to the right. Reads as a redaction line — the “key” that has been struck through. Optional.
Reads as
The Drift Magazine, Aperture, Cabinet, Pirate Wires headline. Quiet but unmistakably edited.
Risk
Source Serif is in wide use on the web for body text — at body size it could read as “tasteful default” rather than “chosen”. Mitigated by the wide tracking + bold weight at masthead scale.

II.
Quietude — the novella imprint

Newsreader, Bold (700), at optical-size 72. Newsreader was drawn by Production Type for long-form reading on the web. At display sizes its terminals soften, the contrast eases, the letters feel read rather than set. It is the most humane of the three. The wordmark is paired with a small burgundy disk — a punctuation mark, not an icon.

III.
Engraved — the fine-art catalogue

Bodoni Moda, Black (900), held between two hairlines. A high-contrast didone — the most assertive of the three. The wide tracking and the over- and under-rules quote 19th-century engraved masthead conventions (Aperture, FRIEZE, museum exhibition catalogues). The “key” mark is a single burgundy v-notch cut into the upper rule — a path opening, not a chevron.

My recommendation

Pick A · Authority as the working master.
Hold C · Engraved in reserve for the Founder Edition cover and the first public poster — where the rules earn their keep.

PICK · A   Source Serif 4, 700, +0.16em — burgundy hairline mark.

Why A and not B?  B is beautiful but it’s the gentler voice. The Founder is selling decision counsel for parents, children, marriages, careers, migration. The reader needs to feel a publication that can hold weight — A holds it. B will be perfect for KEY Letter (Brief 15) when we get there.

Why not C as default?  C is the most beautiful at poster scale and falls apart everywhere else. Reserve it for the cover, not the brand.

Known trade-offs

  1. None of the three has a custom-drawn Y fork in V1. If A is confirmed, V2 will deliver one custom Y with a 6–8% fork in the left arm — drawn as outline, not as filter.
  2. SVG files in this V1 reference webfonts via @import — they render correctly in browsers but are not yet outline-converted for press. V2 will deliver outlines and an embedded subset.
  3. Favicon / OG image / apple-touch-icon assets are deferred to V2, once a direction is chosen. There’s no point producing nine favicons for three directions.

V1 → V2 — what I expect back

  1. Direction selection — A, B, or C.
  2. Tracking confirmation — keep, tighten, or widen by ±0.02em.
  3. Mark verdict — keep, drop, or redesign.
  4. Y fork — go custom, or leave the letterform untouched.
  5. Anything we missed.
