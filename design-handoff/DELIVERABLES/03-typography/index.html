Typography spec

The 70% of the visual difference between a serious publication and an AI landing page is in type. This deliverable picks the families, sets the scale, and writes the micro-rules — the discipline that keeps a page from sliding back into “default web”.

I  ·  DiagnosisReading the code-side stack

The current code is built on Lora + Inter + Noto Serif SC + Noto Sans SC. It works. It does not earn its keep at large sizes.

Lora was drawn for screen body comfort — its letterforms round. At 17 px, set at 1.75 leading, it is excellent. At 56 px (a brief cover) or 96 px (a poster headline), it loses bone. The reader doesn’t register weight; they register pleasantness. KEY needs weight.

Inter is correct for labels and small caps, which is the only role it has in this brief. The Chinese stack — Noto Serif SC and Noto Sans SC — is the right default; nothing in the paid Chinese market improves on it at this price point. I would not change the CJK choices.

My recommendation is to swap Lora → Source Serif 4 and keep everything else. Same delivery channel (Google Fonts), same license, no cost. The system gains an editorial display register without losing any of the body comfort.

VI  ·  Micro-rulesThe discipline

Nine rules. Each is a non-negotiable on the page. Each shows what right looks like next to a description of when to use it.

I. Drop cap
First letter of a manifesto or Brief § I’s opening paragraph only. Never on body. 2 lines deep, 70% leading.

Some decisions can’t be made on impulse. They can’t be outsourced, softened, or reduced to a list of pros and cons. The hardest choices are rarely the one you walked in with.

II. Blockquote
Always italic. 2 pt burgundy left rule, 16 px padding. Used for the reader’s own words quoted back at them.

“If I leave now I’ll lose the equity, but if I stay another year I’ll lose myself— and I keep telling myself those are the same calculation.”

— from the intake of KB-20260213-117

III. Lists
Roman numerals, italic, burgundy. Never bullet dots. The number is part of the prose, not chrome.
  1. Know the context — we remember what you said.
  2. Expose the trade-offs — we surface the costs you can’t un-see.
  3. You decide — the call remains yours.
IV. Punctuation
Smart quotes, em-dash, ellipsis. Never straight quotes. Never --- for em-dash. Chinese punctuation is full-width.

EN  “authored” — not “generated”…    "authored" -- not "generated"...

CN  「决定之前，先找到关键。」    "决定之前,先找到关键."

V. Numerals
Old-style (lowercase) in prose. Tabular (equal-width) in tables. Always for currency and IDs.

Prose: In 2026 the Founder cohort opens to 100 readers, for ¥4,988 over 3 years.

Tabular: Brief No. KB-20260513-001  ·  followup 30 / 90 / 365

VI. Caps tracking
Uppercase Latin gets +0.28em to +0.36em. Line-height 1.4. Sans-serif only. Used for eyebrows, mast labels, footers.

VII. CN + EN mixing
Half-width space between CJK and Latin. Never italicize Chinese. Numbers in Chinese sentences get spaces around them.

VIII. Measure
EN body: 60–75 characters. CN body: 28–42 characters. Anything wider becomes a sea of leading.

EN max-width: 66ch · CN max-width: 32em

A brief is a piece of writing addressed to one reader. The line length must agree.

IX. Paragraph spacing
EN: 1.4–1.6× line-height. CN: 1.6–1.8× line-height. Never first-line indent on the web.

First paragraph. The space between paragraphs is part of the reading rhythm.

Second paragraph. Indent only on print stock that has earned it.

My recommendation

Adopt Plan A as the V1 stack. Swap Lora out for Source Serif 4. Hold Plan B until the site relaunch — and only then if the cohort has earned the licence cost.

PICK · A    Source Serif 4 · Inter · Noto Serif SC · Noto Sans SC · JetBrains Mono

The tokens above are written into typography-css-tokens.css as CSS variables — Claude can @import it directly and the existing Tailwind tokens map onto it cleanly. The change is non-breaking: any element currently using font-serif will pick up Source Serif 4 once the stack swaps.

Known trade-offs

  1. Source Serif is widely used on the web for body text. At body size it could read as “tasteful default”. Mitigated by the wordmark choice (Brief 01) and the wide tracking on labels — the system reads as edited, even when the body font is common.
  2. Chinese leading at 1.88 looks generous on long-form pages and risks looking sparse on cards. We’ll likely tune card-density CN to 1.75 in V2.
  3. Inter is technically a “tech-company sans”. It does no harm here because it never carries a headline; only labels at small sizes. If it ever bothers anyone we swap to Söhne (Plan B).

V1 → V2 — what I expect back

  1. Plan selection — A, B, or C.
  2. Token adjustments — any single step can move ±2 px in size or ±0.05 in leading without disturbing the scale.
  3. Whether to add body-large (19 px) for KEY Letter long-form.
  4. Whether to commit to print-grade Source Serif weights for the printable assets, or stay with regular/bold only.
